Radius — end-to-end build plan

Radius is a neighbourhood index of people, skills and things. A member can rent a ladder, buy a printer, hire an electrician, book two hours of video editing, or post what they need — and the same member can be the one listing. Search runs against the opted-in community inside a chosen radius, not against the web.

This document has three parts, all describing the same six phases: the roadmap (what ships when), the engineering spec (data model and endpoints), and paste-ready build prompts for an AI coding tool. Stack is fixed: Spring Boot on the server, Next.js on the client.

ServerJava 21, Spring Boot 3.3 — Web, Security (JWT), Data JPA, Validation, WebSocket/STOMP, Flyway ClientNext.js 15 App Router, TypeScript, TanStack Query, installable PWA, one responsive codebase for phone and desktop DataPostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS for distance and tsvector for text; S3-compatible object store for photos; Redis for sessions and rate limits MoneyNo fee and no in-app payment until Phase 5. The surfaces exist from Phase 3; the ledger switches on later.

Phase 00 — Foundations

Goal. A deployed shell with real accounts and real locations. No listings yet.

In scope. Phone-number or email sign-up, JWT session, one user row per person carrying both sides of the market. Profile with name, photo, neighbourhood, and a coarse home point stored to about a hundred metres. Skills, teachables, needs and hobbies as free tags. Postgres with PostGIS and Flyway migrations. CI to a staging environment.

Out of scope. Listings, search, any money. No separate owner account — the duality is a capability, not a role.

Done when. A new member signs up on a phone, sets their area, adds four skills, and sees their own profile as others will see it.

Phase 01 — Listings and the nearby feed

Goal. Supply first. Give people a reason to open the app before search exists.

In scope. One listing table covering seven kinds: tool or item to rent, thing to sell, trade service, skill for hire, something taught, space or vehicle, and an open need. Three-step create flow with photos, price and unit, deposit, and a weekly availability pattern. The dashboard where a member sees their own listings and pauses them. The nearby feed — everything within the member's radius, newest and closest first, with category chips over the top. Listing and profile detail pages.

Out of scope. Text search, map, requests.

Done when. A member lists a ladder in four minutes, and a second member two kilometres away sees it in their feed and on the ladder's own page.

Phase 02 — Search, both ways

Goal. Turn a sentence or a set of filters into a ranked list of neighbours and things.

In scope. Two entry points onto one endpoint. Ask takes “I need someone who knows video editing and lives within 5 km”, extracts intent, radius and day, and shows what it understood as removable chips. Filter is category, keyword, distance slider and a free-today toggle. Ranking is text relevance, then distance, then rating. Sort by distance, price or rating. Map view: pins inside the radius ring, tapping a pin opens the listing. Saved searches with a daily digest.

Parsing. Rules first — a synonym dictionary over the tag vocabulary, a distance regex, a date-word list. Cheap, instant, debuggable. Fall back to a language model only when the rules extract nothing, and cache the result by normalised query string.

Done when. The sentence above returns editors within five kilometres, ordered sensibly, in under three hundred milliseconds at the ninety-fifth percentile.

Phase 03 — Requests, booking and chat

Goal. Close the loop. Everything before this is browsing.

In scope. A request carries listing, day, quantity of units, message and a computed amount. Lifecycle: SENT → ACCEPTED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED, with DECLINED, CANCELLED and EXPIRED as exits. Accepting blocks the days on the listing calendar. A chat thread per request over WebSocket, with push and SMS fallback. The price breakdown appears here — rate times units, refundable deposit, and a line reading “Radius fee: none for now”.

Out of scope. Charging anyone. Money changes hands at the handover; the breakdown is a statement of what is owed, not a checkout.

Done when. Two members complete a two-day ladder rental end to end, and the days show as booked to everyone else.

Phase 04 — Trust

Goal. Make a stranger with a drill feel like a neighbour with a drill.

In scope. Government-ID verification through a provider, producing a single badge and no stored document. Two-sided reviews that unlock only after completion and publish together. Rating and completed-count on every card. Report and block, with a moderation queue. Rate limits on new accounts.

Done when. Every card in the feed carries a verification badge and an honest rating, and a reported listing is out of the index within a minute.

Phase 05 — Money

Goal. Switch on the ledger under surfaces people already recognise.

In scope. Payment on acceptance through a gateway. Deposits authorised and held, released on completion, disputed through a claim. Owner payouts to a bank account with a statement. A fee configuration — percentage, cap, and per-category override — that reads zero until the day it does not. Every amount already displayed in Phase 3 keeps its position; only the words under the total change.

Done when. A rental is paid in app, the deposit is held and released, and the owner sees a real payout with the fee itemised.

Indexes that matter. A GiST index on listing.point and profile.home_point; a GIN index on listing.search_vector, refreshed by trigger from title, description and tag labels. Distance filters use ST_DWithin so the index is used; never compute distance in Java and filter afterwards. Store money as integer minor units, never as a floating-point number.

Client structure

One Next.js app serves phone and desktop from the same routes — the phone layout is the prototype in this project, and the desktop layout is the same components at two or three columns. Server components render the feed, detail pages and dashboard; client components own search input, filters, the map, chat and the create flow. TanStack Query handles mutations and cache invalidation. Ship it as an installable PWA before considering native.

Route sketch: / feed · /search · /map · /l/[id] listing · /u/[id] profile · /new create · /requests · /requests/[id] thread · /dashboard · /me.

Part three — build prompts

One prompt per phase, to paste into an AI coding tool in order. Each assumes the previous phase is merged. Send the standing brief first, once.

Standing brief — send once, before Phase 00

We are building Radius: a neighbourhood marketplace where members rent
or buy things and hire each other's skills, inside a chosen radius.
One user account is BOTH customer and provider — never model a separate
owner role.

Stack, fixed: Spring Boot 3.3 on Java 21 (Web, Security with JWT, Data
JPA, Validation, Flyway, WebSocket/STOMP) and PostgreSQL 16 with
PostGIS. Client is Next.js 15 App Router in TypeScript with TanStack
Query, one responsive codebase for phone and desktop, shipped as a PWA.

Rules for every phase: money as integer minor units; distance filters
via ST_DWithin against a GiST index; Flyway migration per change, never
ddl-auto; DTOs at the boundary, entities never leave the service layer;
request state changes only through a service that owns the state
machine; every endpoint gets a slice test.

Work phase by phase. Do not build a later phase's feature early. Ask me
before adding a dependency that is not in the list above.

Phase 00 — Foundations

Phase 00. Set up the monorepo (apps/api, apps/web), Docker Compose with
Postgres and PostGIS, and Flyway.

Server: user, profile, tag, profile_tag tables. Phone OTP sign-up and
JWT sessions with rotating refresh tokens. GET /me, PATCH /me/profile,
and tag add/remove endpoints for the four relations: has, teaches,
needs, enjoys. Store home_point as geography(Point,4326), rounded to
about 100 m before saving.

Client: sign-up, then a profile screen where a member sets name, photo,
area label and radius, and adds tags under those four headings. Mobile
first at 390 px, working up to desktop.

Deliver with slice tests on the auth and profile endpoints, and a
seed script creating twelve members across one neighbourhood.

Phase 01 — Listings and feed

Phase 01. One listing table for seven kinds: RENT_ITEM, SELL_ITEM,
TRADE_SERVICE, SKILL_FOR_HIRE, TEACHING, SPACE_OR_VEHICLE, OPEN_NEED.
Columns per the spec, plus availability_rule for the weekly pattern and
a search_vector maintained by trigger from title, description and tag
labels (GIN index).

Endpoints: create, update, get, my-listings, pause/resume, photo upload
by pre-signed URL then attach. GET /feed?radiusKm&category&cursor —
ST_DWithin against the caller's home_point, keyset pagination, distance
ascending.

Client: the three-step create flow (kind → details → review and
publish), the dashboard listing mine with a live/paused switch, the
nearby feed with category chips, and listing and profile detail pages.

No text search and no requests in this phase.

Phase 02 — Search

Phase 02. POST /search accepting EITHER a raw sentence in q OR
structured filters (category, keyword, radiusKm, availableOn, sort).

Parse a sentence with rules first: a synonym dictionary over the tag
vocabulary, a regex for "within N km", and a date-word list for today,
tomorrow, this weekend. Return the extraction in the response as
{ terms, radiusKm, day, kinds } so the client can show removable chips.
Only if the rules extract nothing, call an LLM, and cache by normalised
query. Rank by ts_rank, then distance, then rating.

Also: GET /search/map?bbox returning pins, clustered server-side above
200 results, and saved searches with a daily digest job.

Client: one search screen with two modes on a segmented control — Ask
(a sentence box, example prompts, parsed chips on the results) and
Filter (category, keyword, distance slider, free-today toggle). Results
sortable by distance, price, rating. A map view with a radius ring,
pins, and a list beneath it.

Phase 03 — Requests, booking, chat

Phase 03. request and message tables. States: SENT, ACCEPTED,
DECLINED, IN_PROGRESS, COMPLETED, CANCELLED, EXPIRED — transitions in a
RequestService, rejected anywhere else, each one audited.

Accepting writes an availability_block over the booked days so the
listing shows those days as taken. SENT expires after 48 hours.

Chat: STOMP over /ws/threads/{requestId}, one thread per request, with
REST history and unread counts. Web push, with SMS as fallback.

Compute the amount on the server: rate x units, plus deposit, plus a
fee line that reads zero. Return it as a breakdown object — the client
never does the arithmetic.

Client: a booking sheet on the listing page (day picker honouring
availability, unit stepper, message, breakdown, send), a confirmation
state, a requests screen split into incoming and outgoing with accept
and decline, and the chat thread.

Still no charging. The breakdown states what is owed at handover.

Phase 04 — Trust

Phase 04. Integrate an ID-verification provider behind a
VerificationProvider interface with a sandbox implementation for tests.
Store only provider_ref, level and verified_at — never a document.

Two-sided reviews: allowed only on a COMPLETED request, hidden until
both sides submit or 14 days pass, then published together. Denormalise
rating average and completed count onto profile for card rendering.

Reports with reasons, a moderation queue, an admin action to unlist, and
rate limits on accounts younger than seven days.

Client: verification badge and rating on every card and profile, a
review prompt after completion, and report and block in a listing menu.

Phase 05 — Money

Phase 05. payment, deposit_hold and payout tables. Charge on
acceptance through the gateway; the webhook is the source of truth and
every write is idempotent by gateway_ref.

Deposits: authorise on acceptance, release on completion, or open a
claim with evidence and an admin decision.

Payouts to the owner's bank account on a schedule, with a statement
itemising each request.

Fees: a FeeConfig with percentage, cap and per-category override, read
at request time and stored on the payment row. It reads zero until we
change it — no code path assumes a non-zero fee.

Client: reuse the Phase 03 breakdown component unchanged; only the words
under the total change from "settled between you" to the paid state.
Add a payouts and statements section to the dashboard.

Two decisions to make before Phase 00

Launch geography. Radius is worthless at low density and good at high density. Pick one or two neighbourhoods and saturate them before opening a second city. Everything in the schema is already scoped by point and radius, so this is a go-to-market choice, not a technical one.

Liability on damage. Phase 05 holds a deposit, which covers a scratched grinder and not a stolen one. Decide before launch whether a rental above a threshold requires a signed handover checklist with photos — it is a small feature in Phase 03 and an expensive retrofit later.
